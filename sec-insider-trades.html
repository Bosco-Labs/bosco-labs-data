Coverage

Today's filings from the EDGAR live feed, minutes after the SEC accepts them; recent quarters from the EDGAR indexes and Form 4 XML; history from 2006 from the SEC's quarterly Insider Transactions Data Sets. Non-derivative table only: options and other derivatives are not included.

Sources, licence and limits

Can I backtest with this without look-ahead bias? Use usable_from, not transaction_date. Insiders report up to two business days after trading, and a Form 4 can be filed until 22:00 New York time, after the close. usable_from is the first weekday after filing_date; enter at the first market open on or after it. Delisted companies are included (look them up by CIK). Before 2023-04-01 Form 4 had no 10b5-1 checkbox, so plan_10b5_1 is null (unknown) for older filings unless a footnote mentions a plan. After that, false can still be a sale under a plan adopted before 2023-02-27, for which the box was optional.

Is this the same data as OpenInsider and similar sites? Same source (SEC Form 4), but you get raw, complete rows through an API, 2006 onward, including amendments (form: "4/A"), joint filings and the 10b5-1 flag.

What about options? Grants, exercises and sales of options and other derivatives sit in the derivative table of Form 4 and are not included. Shares received from an exercise (code M) and sold (code S) are.

Joint filings? One row per transaction; insider_name is the first reporting person, owner_count the number of joint filers, and roles are combined.

Tickers that changed? Tickers are mapped to companies with the SEC's current list. For a delisted or renamed company, pass its CIK, or the old ticker for history (it matches the ticker written in the filings).

Why are some prices empty? Some filings give no price (awards, gifts) or only a footnote; price and value_usd are then null or 0, exactly as filed.

Legal basis? SEC filings are US government public records. See LEGAL.md. This is data, not investment advice.
