Sources, licence and limits

Where does the data come from? Only from the two official APIs of the Office of the Federal Register (National Archives) and the Government Publishing Office: the eCFR versioner API (point-in-time CFR text and version history) and the FederalRegister.gov API (documents since 1994). Both are keyless. The text is converted from XML to plain text and nothing else is changed.

May you resell this? Yes. FederalRegister.gov and eCFR.gov state: "Any person may reproduce or republish any material appearing in any regular or special edition of the Federal Register (1 CFR 2.6). There are no restrictions regarding what is reproduced, who can reproduce it, or where it can be reproduced." Works of the US Government have no copyright (17 U.S.C. 105), and GPO's e-CFR guide says "there are no restrictions on re-use of information in the e-CFR documents". We don't use NARA or OFR seals or CFR logos and don't claim to be an official edition. Sources: About this site, govinfo policies, e-CFR XML guide.

Is the eCFR text the law? It is, in the OFR's words, "authoritative but unofficial". The official CFR is the annual print edition on govinfo.gov, kept current by the daily Federal Register. For legal research, verify against the official editions; each document row links the official govinfo PDF (pdf_url). This is not legal advice.

How exact is the link between a change and a Federal Register rule? The Federal Register indexes documents by CFR part, not section. For each amendment date we list the final rules that affect the same part and took effect (or were published) that day: in practice that is the rule that made the change, but on a busy day it can include a rule that changed a neighbouring section. Each comes with its title and link so an agent can check. Only the 5 newest amendment dates per run are looked up (older ones show fr_documents: null); in a part listing that is each unit's newest change, for the 5 most recent dates. If the Federal Register lookup fails, the row is kept with fr_documents: null and a warning.

How current is it? Live. The eCFR is updated daily and is usually 1-2 days behind the Federal Register (ecfr_up_to_date_as_of); an asOf later than that returns the latest text with a warning. The point-in-time history starts around January 2017; earlier dates fail with the first available date.

Can it diff two versions or return one paragraph? Not yet. A section is returned whole (a citation like 21 CFR 101.9(c)(8) returns all of 101.9 with a warning), and "what changed" is answered by dates and the rules that made the change, not a text diff. Huge appendices such as the Entity List are cut at maxTextChars (raise it up to 500,000).

Which CFR units work? All 50 titles: sections, and appendices or supplements (15 CFR 744 Supplement No. 4, 40 CFR part 60 appendix A-1). Reserved titles fail with a clear message.

Personal data? None is requested. Federal Register documents and CFR appendices can name people or companies (e.g. the Entity List); they are returned only as published.
