Sources, licence and limits

Where does the data come from? Only from the Publications Office of the EU, the publisher of EUR-Lex: its Cellar repository (SPARQL endpoint for metadata and relations, REST content negotiation for texts). Nothing else is added, and the text is not rewritten or summarised.

Is the consolidated text the law? No. Consolidated versions are, in EUR-Lex's own words, "a documentation tool" with no legal effect; only the texts published in the Official Journal are authentic. Every row with a consolidated text carries EUR-Lex's disclaimer in text_notice and links the authentic act in eur_lex_url. Ask with an asOf before the first consolidated version to get the OJ text itself.

Is this legal advice? No. It returns official texts and metadata as published; it does not interpret them. EUR-Lex itself states its information is not professional or legal advice.

Which acts work? Any act with a CELEX number in EUR-Lex: regulations, directives and decisions, corrigenda and consolidated versions. Article extraction works on all current texts; for some old acts (before about 2000) only an HTML text without article markup exists, and the last article may then include trailing headings. Annexes are not split out (use include: ["text"]).

How current is it? Live: every call reads Cellar directly. New OJ acts appear the day they are published; consolidated versions follow when the Publications Office produces them (days to weeks), so latest_consolidated_version_date can lag behind amended_by. in_force can be null for acts published in the last few days, before EUR-Lex has set it.

Why is a title null? Some corrigenda have no title in Cellar, and very new acts may not yet have one in every language.

Points like 6(1)(a)? The paragraph is returned whole; points are lines inside it.

Personal data? None is requested or returned beyond what the published legal acts contain. Signatory names in the metadata are not output.
