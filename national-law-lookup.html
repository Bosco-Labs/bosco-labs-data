Sources, licence and limits

Where does the data come from? UK: legislation.gov.uk (The National Archives), its Legislation API XML. NL: the Basiswettenbestand (BWB) open-data repository of KOOP, the same consolidated texts wetten.overheid.nl shows; names not in our abbreviation list are looked up in the official BWB search service (SRU). Nothing else is added, and the text is not rewritten or summarised.

Is the consolidated text the law? It is an editorial consolidation, not the authentic publication. For Dutch law the authentic text is the publication in the Staatsblad or Staatscourant (Bekendmakingswet, art. 4-5); the row names that publication and links it. UK revised texts are prepared by the legislation.gov.uk editorial team and may not yet include every change: check pending_changes. Every row carries this in text_notice.

Is this legal advice? No. It returns official texts and metadata as published; it does not interpret them.

Is there an English version of Dutch law? No official one. Dutch texts are returned in Dutch; ask your agent to translate and say that the translation is unofficial.

Which acts work? UK: all legislation on legislation.gov.uk with numbered sections, regulations or articles (Acts of the UK, Scottish, Welsh and NI parliaments, SIs, retained EU law such as the UK GDPR). Schedules are not split out yet. NL: every regulation in the BWB (wetten, AMvB's, ministeriële regelingen, beleidsregels), with versions from about 2002 (the start of the BWB's history) onwards.

How current is it? Live: every call reads the source. Dutch versions appear when KOOP publishes them (usually on the day they take effect); legislation.gov.uk applies changes after they are made, so recent amendments can sit in pending_changes for a while.

Points like 172(1)(a)? The subsection is returned whole; points are lines inside it.

Personal data? None is requested or returned beyond what the published legislation contains.
