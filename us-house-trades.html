Coverage

Periodic Transaction Reports of House members filed online, from January 2014 to the latest update of the Clerk's index. Scanned paper PTRs are skipped (their PDF links are listed in the run's SUMMARY record). The Senate and annual financial disclosure reports are not included.

Sources, licence and limits

Which filings are covered? Periodic Transaction Reports of House members filed online, from January 2014 to the latest update of the Clerk's index. Annual financial disclosure reports and the Senate are not included.

What about paper filings? Some PTRs are scanned paper forms (about 12% in 2026, more than half in 2014). They have no text, so they are skipped rather than guessed; the run log and the SUMMARY record list their PDF links so you can read them yourself.

Why is the ticker empty? The ticker is taken only from the filing, when the member wrote it in parentheses. Bonds, Treasury bills, private funds and many older filings have none. Nothing is looked up or guessed.

Why is asset_type empty on old rows? Filings before about 2018 carry no asset type code. Options are still recognised from their description; other rows stay null.

Amendments? is_amendment is true when the transaction was filed as an amendment. Its filing_date is the amendment's date, not when the trade first became public, so days_to_disclose overstates the delay for those rows.

Why upper/lower case oddities? Some older PDFs garble the case of the asset text (e.g. (bAbA)). asset_description is kept as filed; ticker is upper-cased.

How fresh? As fresh as the Clerk's yearly index, which is regenerated regularly: the copy fetched on 2026-11-05 was dated 2026-11-04 and listed filings up to 2026-11-03.

Is this trading advice? No. This is a copy of public disclosures, with ranges instead of exact amounts and trades that become public weeks after they happen.

Legal? These are public disclosures published by the Clerk under the STOCK Act. Users are responsible for complying with applicable law on the use of these disclosures, including 5 U.S.C. 13107.
