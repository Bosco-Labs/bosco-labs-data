Why is financials empty? See not_found: the company may file no machine-readable accounts (paper/PDF filings, very small companies, countries without an open accounts register), or it is a US company that files no 10-K (US banks: see bank.financials). Unlisted French companies are not covered: INPI's annual-accounts API needs an account, and the open INPI-derived "Ratios financiers" dataset files figures under the wrong fiscal year (checked against Danone's ESEF reports), so it is not used. Most Finnish companies file paper or PDF accounts, which are not open data.

Why is revenue null? The company didn't tag it: small UK and Danish companies often publish no profit and loss account or only gross profit. employees is usually null for ESEF reports, which rarely tag it.

A name gave the wrong company. Names are matched against GLEIF's register of legal entities (companies with an LEI; with country NO, FR, FI, EE, IE, CZ or LV also the national register). When several entities share the name, a listed company (one filing ESEF reports) goes first, and candidates and a warning say there was a choice; without country that happens for any name shared by several entities. Check candidates, add country, or pass the LEI or registration number, which always resolve exactly. A name that resembles no entity (a typo, a company without an LEI, a made-up name) gives no report and no charge: the message lists the three closest names with their similarity score so you can pick one by its LEI or number. Write a US ticker in capitals (COKE) or with $; Coke is searched as a name.

Are figures consolidated? ESEF reports (listed companies) are consolidated group figures. National register accounts are the company's own statutory accounts; for Norway they are explicitly the company's own accounts, not the group's. financials.basis says which.

Insider buys and sells? This version counts Form 4 filings in the last 90 days (buys and sells together). For each transaction, use SEC Insider Trades.

Need more? Full statements with 16-25 metrics per year: Company Financials API (UK, DK, NO) and EU Annual Reports API (ESEF). Every SEC filing: SEC Filings Feed.

Personal data? Company-level data, plus one exception for KYB: for UK companies the people section lists the current officers and persons with significant control as Companies House publishes them (name, role, appointment or notification date, nationality, country of residence, month and year of birth, nature of control). Their addresses and occupations are never output, and resigned officers and ceased PSCs are left out; leave people out of include to skip it. The opt-in appointments section adds each current officer's other company appointments (company, role, dates), read live and never stored; charges lists the lenders named in each charge; insolvency outputs notice type, date, company name and link only, never the people named in a notice. For Polish companies people lists the board, proxies and shareholders exactly as the Ministry of Justice's open KRS API serves them: people's names already masked to a first letter and asterisks, never a PESEL, no marital fields, no free-text proxy scopes; trustees and liquidators are not listed. Polish data is read live per call and not stored. For Norwegian companies people lists the CEO, board and other role holders from the Brønnøysund roles API, and for French companies the directors (dirigeants of natural persons) from the RNE, each with name, role and birth month only (since 2026-11-08). Outside the UK, Poland, Norway and France, names of directors, officers, board members, auditors or shareholders are never requested or output (Finland's open data has no persons, and the address's c/o line is dropped; the Estonian persons, shareholder and beneficial-owner files are never downloaded). Estonian sole traders (FIE) are left out of the index and name search, and a lookup by their code fails without a charge. Sole traders are natural persons: Norwegian ones are excluded from name search, French ones (entrepreneurs individuels) from name search and from lookups by SIREN, which fail without a charge; Finland's and Ireland's open data hold none; Czech natural persons in business and Latvian individual merchants, individual enterprises and farms are excluded from name search, and a lookup by their number fails without a charge. Sole proprietors with an LEI (GLEIF category SOLE_PROPRIETOR, any country) are never candidates and are refused without a charge.

How fresh is it? Live: every call reads the sources directly (GLEIF is updated several times a day, registers when companies file). Exception: Estonia's legal form, VAT number, activity and figures come from a weekly index of the register's daily open-data files (the date is in sources[].name); name, status and address are read live. Irish and Latvian records come from the registers' daily open-data snapshots (up to a day old); Czech records are read live from ARES. FDIC figures are quarterly (Call Reports).

How far can I trust status for Ireland, the Czech Republic and Latvia? Ireland: it is the CRO's own status word; receiverships are not in the open data. Czech Republic: ARES has no status field, so it is derived: "insolvency proceedings" means the Insolvency Register holds the company, not which stage the case is in, and companies deleted before 2012 are not found at all. ARES data is informative only and, in the ministry's words, cannot serve as evidence in court. Latvia: derived from three open datasets; if the liquidation or insolvency data does not answer, no register status is given and a warning says so. When the Polish, Irish, Czech or Latvian register has no record of the number, status says so ("not in the current register") and is never GLEIF's; for a French SIREN that Sirene has no public record of it is "not found in Sirene (unknown, or its record is not public)". Norway: a procedure flag in the Brønnøysund record gives its own status ("bankrupt", "being wound up", "in reconstruction negotiation", or "under a procedure the register flags (...)" for one we do not name), never "active". A US bank the FDIC lists as no longer active gets "inactive at the FDIC (...)", whatever its LEI record says. When the best name match is the one company among namesakes with ESEF filings, it is the pick and its entry in candidates has "listed_pick": true. Where status comes from GLEIF, a name the law marks as in liquidation or insolvency ("in liquidatie", "i.L.", "in Insolvenz", "en liquidation") gives that status, since GLEIF keeps such a company ACTIVE. Spain is not covered beyond GLEIF: its gazette (BORME) lists daily notices by company name and cannot answer "what is this company's status" (see LEGAL.md).
