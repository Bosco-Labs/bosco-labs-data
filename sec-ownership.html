Sources, licence and limits

How current is 13F data? 13F reports are quarterly and due 45 days after quarter end, so holdings are up to 4.5 months old when they appear. Positions are what the manager reported: long US-listed equities, options and convertibles on the SEC's 13(f) list; no shorts, no non-US holdings, no cash.

How current is N-PORT data? Funds report holdings monthly, but only the report for the last month of each fiscal quarter is made public, 60 days after that month ends. A fund's period_of_report is therefore its own fiscal quarter end (ARKK: 2026-07-31; VOO: 2026-06-30), and the holders of a stock can be as of different month ends (each row says which). N-PORT covers registered funds and ETFs (open-end funds, closed-end funds, most ETFs), not money market funds or unit investment trusts such as SPY; it includes bonds, cash vehicles, derivatives and short positions (payoff_profile).

Values? As reported, in US dollars at quarter end (reports filed before 2023 used $1,000s and are converted). Amendments are applied: a restatement replaces the original report, a "new holdings" amendment adds to it.

Tickers? 13F filings carry CUSIPs, not tickers, and the SEC publishes no CUSIP-to-ticker list. The company is found by its CIK or, on 13F rows, its name in the SEC's ticker list (renamed or oddly abbreviated names stay null). The share class comes from the CUSIP (Alphabet class C 02079K107 is GOOG, class A 02079K305 is GOOGL; likewise Fox, News Corp, Under Armour, Zillow and the Liberty tracking stocks) or from the class in title_of_class when the ticker names it (BRK-A / BRK-B, LEN / LEN-B, HEI / HEI-A). When a company has several listed classes and the row doesn't say which, ticker is null and ticker_candidates lists the company's tickers; a ticker filter matches those too. cusip is always the exact security.

Which funds hold a ticker: which CUSIP? The ticker's CUSIPs are read from the company's recent Schedule 13D/13G filings, which can cover several share classes; CUSIPs of another class (GOOGL when you ask for GOOG) are left out and named in the status message. Pass cusip for one exact security, or when the company has no recent 13D/13G.

A name matched the wrong fund? Manager names (filer) go through EDGAR's company search; the status message lists the other matches. Pass the CIK as filer to be exact. Fund names (fund) are matched with the SEC's yearly series and class list: case, punctuation and words like "fund", "index", "trust", "ETF" and share classes ("Admiral Shares", "Class K") are ignored; an exact name wins, then names containing all your words, funds with a ticker and shorter names first. The status message names the fund picked and up to 3 alternatives; pass the ticker or series ID to be exact. Funds launched after the list's yearly update (June) are found by ticker only.

Older 13D/13G? Only filings since 2024-12-18, when Schedule 13D/13G became structured XML. For older ones, use our SEC EDGAR Filings Actor for the filing links.

13D vs 13G? A 13D is filed by a holder of more than 5% who may seek to influence the company (activist); a 13G by passive holders and institutions. percent_of_class is the highest among the filing's reporting persons; all of them are in reporting_persons.

Form 144? A notice by an insider or affiliate of a planned sale of restricted or control stock, filed when the order is placed. It is not proof that the sale happened (Form 4 reports that: see our SEC Insider Trading Actor). Not every Form 144 is followed by a sale.

Legal basis? SEC filings are US government public records; see LEGAL.md. This is data, not investment advice.
