Sources, licence and limits

How fresh is the data? Same-evening rows are in by about 7 p.m. New York on the day the Pentagon announces them (weekdays). The USAspending history is months behind for DoD: contract actions are published in FPDS, and so on USAspending, with a delay of up to 90 days (a DoD rule for operational security). On 2026-11-05 the DoD data was complete up to 2026-07-23, a lag of about 105 days; later dates only held a few hundred actions (mainly Army Corps of Engineers civil works). That is why the default window is the last 135 days, and why every row carries a data_lag_note. Civilian agencies report within days. Old dates keep filling in, so use onlyNewSinceLastRun on a schedule instead of re-reading the same window.

How does the same-evening list get in? The Pentagon e-mails its daily contract list around 5 p.m. New York. Each weekday evening we read that e-mail, turn every awardee in it into a row and store it; your run reads the stored rows (no e-mail or AI call happens in your run). Rows are matched to tickers the same strict way as the history.

The list is parsed by AI. Can I trust it? The prose is turned into rows by Claude (Haiku), with checks around it: the model has no tools and is told the text is data, not instructions; its answer must be exactly a JSON list in a fixed schema with valid types, or that part of the day is rejected and read again; every row's company name and dollar amount must literally appear in one announcement paragraph, or the row is dropped; and its contract number, completion date, obligated amount and labels must be backed by that paragraph, or they are null. That stops made-up rows, but the model can still occasionally pick the wrong one of two amounts in a paragraph, or the wrong city. For anything you act on, open source_url (the war.gov article) and check.

Why do several companies show the same amount? It is one multiple-award contract. When eleven firms "are awarded a $975,000,000 contract", they compete for orders up to $975M together: $975M is a shared ceiling, not $975M each, and nobody knows yet how it will be split (so we don't pro-rate it). Each firm still gets its own row with the full amount, marked shared_award: true, awardees_count: 11, amount_basis: "ceiling", the other ten in co_awardees, and one award_group_id for all eleven. Never sum amount_usd over these rows: keep one row per award_group_id (eleven rows summed would claim $10.7B). Even then a ceiling is a maximum, not money committed; obligated_at_award_usd says what was actually obligated when the announcement states it.

What do award_kind, pricing_type and amount_basis mean? award_kind says what the row is: new_contract, modification, option_exercise, task_order, delivery_order, multiple_award_idiq, single_award_idiq, bpa_call or other. pricing_type is the contract type (firm_fixed_price, cost_plus_fixed_fee, cost_plus_award_fee, cost_plus_incentive_fee, fixed_price_incentive, time_and_materials, cost_reimbursable, hybrid for several, other). amount_basis says what amount_usd measures: ceiling, face_value, modification_value, obligated or estimated; is_ceiling is kept and is true exactly when it is ceiling. For the daily list the AI's label is only kept when a word that supports it ("multiple award", "exercises an option", "firm-fixed-price", ...) is in the announcement paragraph, and that word is shown in classification_evidence; otherwise the field is null, never guessed. For USAspending rows they come from the official codes:

USAspending`award_kind`
Award type D, DEFINITIVE CONTRACT (day with the base award)new_contract
Award type B, PURCHASE ORDER (base award)new_contract
Award type C, DELIVERY ORDER (base award; FPDS files task orders here too)delivery_order
Award type A, BPA CALL (base award)bpa_call
Modification with action type G (exercise an option)option_exercise
Any other modificationmodification

USAspending rows are always amount_basis: "obligated", shared_award: false and pricing_type: null (the search the Actor uses does not return the pricing type). IDIQ and BPA vehicles themselves carry no obligations, so the *_idiq kinds come from the daily list only; orders under them show the vehicle in parent_award_id.

Is this the same as the DoD's daily 5 p.m. contract announcements? Same contract actions, two records. dod_daily_list rows come from the announcement itself, the same evening, with face values. usaspending rows arrive months later with the obligated amount of that day (often lower than the face value), UEI, parent company, NAICS and PSC. The Actor does not merge them: the same award can appear once from each source.

Why do amounts differ from the press release? For USAspending rows: obligations vs. face value (see above). Also, one row sums all actions on that contract that day, and modifications can be negative.

Why is my company's ticker missing? The recipient name and its parent must match the SEC name exactly after cleaning (the daily list names no parent, so only the awardee's own name counts there). Joint ventures, foreign parents and renamed subsidiaries are missed on purpose. Search by recipients (name contains) to see them anyway.

Why is shares_outstanding empty for a matched ticker? Companies with several share classes (e.g. Palantir) or that don't report the cover-page count as a single number, and counts older than 400 days from the signing date, are left empty rather than guessed.

Is the ticker the company that was listed at the time? Matching uses today's SEC list and today's parent company from USAspending. A subsidiary sold since then may show its current owner's ticker; companies that are no longer listed are not matched.

Is this investment advice? No. It is a data product: public contract records joined to public SEC identifiers. No prices, no market caps, no signals.
