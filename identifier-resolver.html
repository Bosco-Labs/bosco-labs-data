Input fields

FieldTypeMeaning
identifierstring (required)ONE identifier of a security or company: an ISIN ("US0378331005"), an LEI ("HWUPKR0MPOU8FGXBT394"), an SEC CIK ("320193"), a ticker ("AAPL", "BRK.B"; add exchange for non-US markets), a FIGI ("BBG000B9XRY4") or a company name ("Siemens AG"). idTypestringWhat the identifier is. Default "auto": digits only = CIK, 20 characters = LEI, 12 characters = ISIN or FIGI (by check digit), up to 6 characters without spaces = ticker (read as a name if no US ticker matches), anything else = company name. Empty (default) = US tickers from the SEC's list. With an exchange, an all-digit identifier is read as a ticker ("7974" + "JT" = Nintendo), not a CIK.

Sources, licence and limits

Where does the data come from? Four official or openly licensed sources, called live: GLEIF (LEI records and the ISIN-to-LEI map), OpenFIGI (FIGIs and listings), the SEC (CIKs and US tickers) and ESMA's FIRDS register (CFI code, instrument name, EU trading venues).

Can I reuse the output? The sources allow it, with these terms, quoted: - GLEIF: "The data available through the Access Service are provided under the CC0 licence". You must not create the impression that the data or this service are "provided or supported or authorized" by GLEIF. GLEIF calls its ISIN-to-LEI files "open source" and names no separate licence for them; this Actor reads them through the same API. - OpenFIGI: Bloomberg "hereby dedicates FIGI Identifiers to the public domain", including "redistribution of the FIGI Identifiers to your customers". Names, tickers and exchange codes come with OpenFIGI's "as is" disclaimer. - SEC: "Information presented on sec.gov is considered public information and may be copied or further distributed". - ESMA: reproduction is "authorised ... provided the source is acknowledged", and where it is sold "the publisher must inform buyers that it may be obtained free of charge through ESMA website". So: the FIRDS fields in this output are available free of charge from ESMA at https://registers.esma.europa.eu/publication/searchRegister?core=esma_registers_firds. You pay for the join, the validation and the speed, not for ESMA's data. ESMA does not endorse this service.

What is not covered? CUSIP and SEDOL (licensed identifiers; not used or returned). FIRDS covers only instruments admitted to or traded on EU/EEA venues, so a US-only small cap has no CFI code or venues. CIKs exist only for SEC registrants. FIRDS is searched by ISIN, LEI and share name only (one query each, no bulk download); derivatives with thousands of venue records are cut at 500 records.

How good is name matching? Best with the full legal name ("Siemens AG", "Toyota Motor Corporation"). A name is matched to an exact SEC company name, else to the issuer whose shares carry that name on the most EU venues, else to an exact GLEIF name. match_confidence, match_note and alternatives say how sure the match is; for certainty pass an ISIN or LEI.

What if OpenFIGI is busy? OpenFIGI allows 25 mapping requests a minute per caller. Answers are cached for 6 hours. When the limit is hit, the row still comes back with the GLEIF, SEC and FIRDS fields, FIGI fields null and a warning saying when to retry. A FIGI or a non-US ticker can only be resolved through OpenFIGI, so those fail (no charge) while it is limited.

How fresh is it? Live on every call: GLEIF updates daily, FIRDS publishes daily, the SEC ticker list is cached for at most 6 hours per run.

Personal data? None. GLEIF records of sole proprietors (natural persons) are never used for name matches.

Is this investment advice? No. It returns identifiers and reference data as the sources publish them.
